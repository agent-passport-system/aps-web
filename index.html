Agent Passport System

APS gives every AI agent a verifiable identity, ties it to the person or company it represents, and signs every action it is allowed to take.

Proof that travels with the action, not a log you check after something breaks.

02 Why this matters

AI agents are starting to act for people and companies. They move money, touch data, call tools, and trigger workflows.

When a person acts on behalf of a company, trust is built in. They have a title, a mandate, and an approval chain. You know what they can sign for, and who answers if it goes wrong. When software acts on behalf of a company, none of that is attached. The action happens. The proof does not.

The next trust boundary is not the user login. It is the action an agent takes on your behalf.

03 The missing piece

Access is not authority. A login authorizes a session. APS authorizes an action.

A token proves a system was allowed through the door. It does not prove the agent had the right to make a specific decision, at that time, under that policy, for that principal.

A log shows that something occurred. Neither answers the question that matters later. Was the agent allowed to do that?

04 What APS is

A verifiable identity, and a signed receipt for every action an agent is allowed to take.

I
Identity
Which agent acted, and which principal it represents.
S
Scoped authority
The limits, budget, and conditions it operates under, enforced at runtime.
E
Evidence
A signed receipt for every permitted action, and evidence for every denial.

APS does not replace identity providers, wallets, or orchestration. It sits beside the protocols you already run, as the governance layer.

05 How it works

Authority flows down. Evidence flows back.

Principal
The person or company behind the agent
→
Passport
Verifiable identity, tied to the principal
→
Delegation
Scoped authority, with limits and conditions
→
Gateway
Checks scope, policy, and freshness
→
Action
The operation runs, or is denied
→
Receipt
Signed record, or evidence of denial
←Evidence flows back to the principal

Each step down the chain narrows what the agent can do. Each action returns a signed artifact the principal can verify later.

06 Why it holds

A log is a diary. A receipt is evidence.

A log says something happened. A receipt states who authorized it, what scope applied, what policy evaluated it, what action occurred, and what evidence remains.

07 The long bet

Identity is where this starts. The harder problem is this.

Beneficiary attribution

When an agent creates work, moves value, or produces an outcome, the person or company behind it remains the principal who participated through it. Earned and traceable, not pooled or redistributed.

APS makes that participation provable.

08 Why open, and how it sustains

Trust cannot be proprietary.

Agents cross tools, clouds, wallets, payment rails, and jurisdictions. If the layer that records and verifies authority is owned by one company, the agent economy inherits that company's incentives and risk.

Open at the edge. Strict at the core.

The protocol is the adoption. The gateway is the moat. Enterprises do not buy a standard. They buy enforcement, verification, audit export, and integration. The AEOESS gateway turns receipts into the audit-ready evidence the EU AI Act and enterprise buyers increasingly require.
