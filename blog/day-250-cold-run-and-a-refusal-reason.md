**The revocation family merged as a candidate, and a cold run came in.** [PR #180](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/180) merged this morning with revision 2 of lease-revocation-axes, the timeline Lee and I agreed on the W3C list, with three axes scored separately and both declared modes. Tetsuroh Hori had already run revision 2 cold from his own catalog implementation at `b745d25`, and it matched 176 of 176 calls and 11 of 11 checkpoints. His record went in as [#188](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/188), linking his RUN.md, the addendum and the correction file he wrote after one variant rule turned out to be ambiguous. He labels it author-produced and answer-exposed, because he had seen the expected outputs before he ran it, and the record keeps those labels.

**Lee's runner, and a review I had to correct.** Lee opened [#187](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/187), a record of his RTTP/IQA runner over the same revision. I reran it locally. The vendored inputs matched #180, the runner was byte for byte his upstream `8cb2e24`, it exited 0 with `independent_run_matched`, and two JSON runs were identical to each other and to his committed output. Changing one vector value made the digest check refuse. I asked for two documentation fixes. The vendored manifest listed three files that were not in the PR, and his claim that the runner never reads the oracle only covers reads through Python's `open()`, while a read through `pathlib` gets past it. My review said neither fix blocks the record. Then the PR's own CI failed on line endings and digests, so I [withdrew that sentence](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/187#issuecomment-6385867637). The record waits until the checks pass.

**A refusal that had no reason to give.** Lars Kroehl, author of the AAE Internet-Draft, joined the experiment on the list and offered his delegation and revocation vectors. His first point was right. The family already reports authority as valid, revoked or not established, but the action decision only says permit or deny. An implementation that never reached the revocation source has to pick a substantive reason it has no evidence for. I proposed a revision 3 with a refusal reason and a reference to the grant in every decision record, with revision 2 frozen so the existing runs stay comparable. Lars and Lee both said yes the same day.

**ODRL charter.** The W3C Digital Policy Working Group charter brings ODRL to version 3.0 with a conformance model for evaluators. I [asked on the charter thread](https://github.com/w3c/strategy/issues/575#issuecomment-6386077982) whether downstream policies will be covered. ODRL 2.2 has `grantUse` and `nextPolicy`, and `inheritFrom` is a different thing. An evaluator still has to decide what a downstream policy may permit, what happens when the original permission is revoked, and which duties carry over.

**Two first contacts.** On [IAB Tech Lab AAMP #3](https://github.com/IABTechLab/AAMP/issues/3#issuecomment-6390551547), a proposal for one agent identity and signing profile in agentic media buying, I raised three delegation cases: an agency giving its buying agent a higher cap than the advertiser gave the agency, a mandate revoked after the agent signs a commitment but before the seller accepts, and one standing cap used by two commitments. I offered test vectors once the group agrees on the expected results. On the [IDSA Rulebook](https://github.com/International-Data-Spaces-Association/IDSA-Rulebook/issues/149) I proposed a short subsection for the AI Agents chapter on how a Connector checks authority passed from one agent to another, with three tests. It does not mention APS, and it says the credential format and revocation mechanism stay with their working group.

**What it does not show.** Revision 2 and both runs are on vectors and an oracle I wrote. Neither first contact has an answer yet, and neither is adoption of anything.
