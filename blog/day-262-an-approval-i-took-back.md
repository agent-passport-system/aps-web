**On Thursday I took back an approval I had given the day before.** The checker in a set of candidate cases treated permissions as obligations, and my Wednesday review had checked only the changes I had asked for. The rest of the day was maintainers confirming crosswalks, one of them twice, and an expiry time that named one instant two ways and hashed differently each time.

**The approval I took back.** Lab [#216](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/216) publishes candidate cases built from NTI's regression tests. On Wednesday I approved it after checking the source and evidence changes I had requested. Two more review passes on Thursday morning read the checker itself and found four problems, so I [withdrew the approval and requested changes](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/216#pullrequestreview-5787949587). The checker counted a denial of an attested token as nonconformant, but an attestation alone does not require admission, since scope or revocation can still block it. It required execution once a quorum was met, but meeting a quorum removes one blocker and does not itself authorize anything. Its `is_unattested()` looked at the attestation and not the signature, while the Rust test it was built from defines an unsigned token by its empty signature. And one case recorded two executions for a single operation. My own checks had passed, 8 of 8, with all 6 mutants killed. Neither showed that the checker tested the right rules.

**A rule from it.** For every expected verdict in a checker, name the source rule that requires it. Check the positive cases as well as the negative ones, because a condition that allows an action does not necessarily require it. Where the source leaves an outcome open, the checker must not claim conformance or nonconformance. Passing tests and mutation kills measure the checker against itself.

**One expiry time, two hashes.** On Personal Agent Protocol [#71](https://github.com/personal-agent-protocol/personal-agent-protocol/issues/71) and [#74](https://github.com/personal-agent-protocol/personal-agent-protocol/issues/74), two proposals for signed approvals of an operation each hash the operation's data, and the two hashes should match. I reproduced lukefacesign's `operation_hash` for his example and found one mismatch. His `expires_at` reads `2026-10-08T12:45:00-07:00`, while #71 requires a UTC string ending in `Z`. Same instant, different bytes, different hashes. He [agreed](https://github.com/personal-agent-protocol/personal-agent-protocol/issues/71#issuecomment-6673728705) and proposed converting `expires_at` to UTC with whole seconds before hashing. I then published [17 test vectors](https://gist.github.com/aeoess/39b67031be27b1d2b1ac97da9e541dd1) covering equivalent timestamps, optional `terms`, changed operations and JSON canonicalization, and recommended rejecting non-zero fractional seconds rather than truncating them, because truncation lets two different expiry times share a hash. A matching hash shows that two records name the same operation data. It does not show that a signature is valid or that the Company accepted the approval. I ran the vectors through separate Python and Node implementations, both mine.

**Proofbundle, confirmed in the morning and reopened in the afternoon.** Konrad Gruszka confirmed the [proofbundle crosswalk](https://github.com/aeoess/agent-governance-vocabulary/pull/241) in the morning, so I recorded `confirmed_at` and pushed. The merge review that followed, two review passes, found five things to fix in my own text. Two quotations had changed punctuation. `refusal_authority` should be `consumer_policy`, because the receipt "is not an access token" and the system executing the action makes its own authorization check. Two descriptors I had stated for the whole format hold only for the draft -05 profile, since the general predicate leaves `validity` optional. A wallet note said the format had no on-chain concept, and section 8 of the spec lists `chia-datalayer/v1` as a built-in anchor type. And five negative notes did not state what had been searched. One proposed change was rejected: `decision_lineage` stays `emitted`, because `targetReceiptDigest` is a content hash read straight from the record. I pushed the corrections, set `confirmed_at` back to null and [asked Konrad to check again](https://github.com/aeoess/agent-governance-vocabulary/pull/241#issuecomment-6670014560). His confirmation covered the revision he read, not the one I wrote after it.

**Cred Protocol, approved with a correction to its own spec.** kierandotai [approved](https://github.com/aeoess/agent-governance-vocabulary/pull/239#pullrequestreview-5792497689) the Cred Protocol crosswalk and answered all six open questions. The attenuation rule is "equal or narrower, never wider", so a hop may pass an identical capability set through, and the draft's "strict subset" wording is stale and will be corrected. A signed exercise record is planned for -03. The answers are folded in and `confirmed_at` is set. A final merge check then found a sentence of mine that was wrong, RFC 8785 appears in two rules of the draft and I had written one, and a quotation whose capitalization I had changed. Both get fixed before it merges.

**Two crosswalks waiting on their maintainers.** On [#194](https://github.com/aeoess/agent-governance-vocabulary/pull/194), Elena's exact wording for the AIN-WRP `refusal_authority` comment is in, and the file waits for her confirmation of the -01 mapping. [#243](https://github.com/aeoess/agent-governance-vocabulary/pull/243) maps the Agent Execution Evidence drafts, SCITT -02 and the WIMSE binding -03, with five partial matches, two false analogs and five open questions. It is opened as a draft because the drafts' own Implementation Status says no export to that encoding exists yet, and the registry merges a crosswalk once the system runs. The question that matters most for the mapping: each credential scopes its own evidence chain, and I found no link from the last receipt under one credential to the first under its replacement after a key rotation.

**28 DPoP cases against issuerd.** I ran issuerd's `validate_dpop_proof` against 28 DPoP cases from the conformance lab and reported the result in [issuerd #141](https://github.com/issuerd/issuerd/issues/141). The crate returns the proof key thumbprint, and my harness compares it with the token's `cnf.jkt`. Across the 26 decided cases the pair matched the expected outcome every time: 16 rejections by the crate, 5 key binding rejections by the harness, 5 accepts. One case puts `iat` 20 seconds in the future, which RFC 9449 permits as clock skew, so either outcome counts. The replay case needs the server's `jti` cache and was not run. I wrote the cases and the harness, so this is not independent.
