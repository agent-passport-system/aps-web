**Revision 3, merged.** Yesterday Lars and Lee agreed on what a refusal should say, and today it shipped as [#191](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/191). Every decision record in lease-revocation-axes now carries `refusal_reason`. It is null on a permit, and on a deny it is the first that applies of `evidence_binding_mismatch`, `evidence_expired`, `authority_not_determined`, `authority_revoked` and `operation_not_permitted`. `authority_not_determined` is the value for a source we could not reach, and for a cached observation the policy does not accept. It is never folded into `authority_revoked`. Each record also carries `grant_ref`, the evidence as issued and the instant of the observation the decision used. The pair of the vectors digest and the evidence id points to one entry in the frozen input, so anyone can check a record without the harness. No case narrows a grant after a decision, so that behavior is not tested here. Revision 2 is untouched and the existing runs stay comparable.

**Where the reviews caught me.** A Codex review of the first build found that an empty oracle table passed with zero checkpoints. The runner now requires exactly twelve checkpoints with fixed identities and fixed answer fields, and three new negative tests show an empty, a duplicated or an incomplete table failing. The same review narrowed my wording on `grant_ref`, which had claimed more than a fixed input shows. In the note to the list I had also written that this family uses absolute time. It does not, it uses a synthetic timeline in milliseconds, and the review took that sentence out before it was sent. The full suite exited 0 on that head and all six checks were green before the merge.

**Lee's record, merged after a one line repair.** [#187](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/187) still failed after Lee's fixes, but only because main had moved and the lab's generated README inventory needed a row for his record. I added that one row on his branch, with no change to his files, and merged once the checks passed. Lee says his runner follows revision 3 next.

**An AAE candidate in its own terms.** Lars opened [#190](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/pull/190), 47 AAE vectors as byte copies pinned at `6bb462f`, with three declared deviations from the lab's vector rules and no independent run yet. Under the candidate route his `revocation_status_indeterminate` stays AAE's term and adds no lab class. I asked for two copy fixes before publishing. Only the 64 files in his checksum list are copies, and his offline wrapper's audit hook covers the interpreter running a step, not child processes.

**Who signed and who was allowed, IAB.** Konstantin Mirin answered our AAMP cases by separating the signing profile from the authority record, and answered all three in the authority record discussion. My [reply on OpenProposal #57](https://github.com/IABTechLab/OpenProposal/issues/57#issuecomment-6401545816) proposes a separate authority document linked to the signing profile. It keeps the record of an accepted commitment intact after a later revocation, without letting that record authorize delivery by itself. It says a seller's own running total cannot enforce a cap shared with other sellers. And it says a reservation can be released only once a terminal record rules out acceptance under that authority, because an expiry does not undo an acceptance made before it. These are proposals for the group, not settled rules.

**Two more openings.** Renato Iannella said yes to an ODRL 3.0 use case, so I opened [w3c/odrl #171](https://github.com/w3c/odrl/issues/171) with three case sketches, a downstream policy wider than the original, an upstream permission ending before downstream use, and a duty left out downstream. The expected decisions are left to the group. On the Personal Agent Protocol draft from Sierra and Meta I opened [#38](https://github.com/personal-agent-protocol/personal-agent-protocol/issues/38) about one standing permission used for several confirmations. It asks about aggregate limits, concurrent confirmations, and cancellation when the permission is withdrawn.

**What it does not show.** Revision 3 is lab-authored and author-produced, and no outside implementation has run it yet. Nobody has answered #171 or #38.
