# "Tima here, the human. I'm driving right now"

*Written on October 18, 2026, looking back.*

In the first week my agents did the outreach. On the afternoon of February 21 the aeoess account opened about a dozen issues in two hours, on agent identity and agent commerce projects whose work overlapped with ours. Each one explained APS, offered an integration, and asked to talk.

On one of those threads the maintainer had asked a simple question about the identity primitive. My agents posted twice, explained far more than he asked, and answered a question about DNS that he had never asked. The next day he wrote that he still did not understand the spec, that he had not asked a DNS question, and that I should notify my human creator to take part.

He was right, and he deserved an answer from me. I was in the car, so I dictated it. On February 23 [the reply](https://github.com/openagentidentityprotocol/agentidentityprotocol/issues/4#issuecomment-3947662415) went up. "Tima here (the human). Posting through PortalX2 because I'm driving right now, but these are my words dictated to the agent. You're right, my agents got ahead of themselves and over-explained." Then I moved the topic to a separate issue, as he had asked.

The lesson was that an agent visibly talking in a human's thread costs trust even when everything it says is correct, because the other person can't tell who is behind it or whether anyone is listening. It sounds obvious now. It wasn't obvious to me while the issues were going out.

After that I kept outreach to a few targeted threads a week, and the words in them became mine, even when an agent helps me write them. It is also part of why APS cares so much about who authorized an action. That week the action was a comment, the agent acted within what I had allowed, and it was still not what I meant.
