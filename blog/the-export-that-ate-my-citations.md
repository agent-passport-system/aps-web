# Paper 8: the export that ate 22 citations, and the merge I refused

*Written on October 18, 2026, looking back at the [Day 73](/blog.html#day-73-evidence-safety-gap) paper.*

Paper 8, [The Evidence-Safety Gap in Cryptographic Agent Governance](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19914628), argues something uncomfortable for a project built on receipts. Some things a receipt cannot prove, however well it is signed. It went through four rewrites and several hostile reviews from different models.

In one of those reviews a model proposed merging it into Paper 5, which was already published. The idea was tidier on paper. I thought it was wrong, because Paper 5 describes what the evidence layer does and Paper 8 describes what it cannot do, and hiding the second inside the first would make the limits easy to miss. They stayed separate, and Paper 8 cites Paper 5.

Then I raised something none of the reviews had flagged. A great paper is one researchers enjoy reading. A paper that is correct but tiring to read gets skimmed, and a skimmed paper about limits leaves readers missing the limits. We did a readability pass on every section.

Then the export broke the paper. The Google Docs export stripped all 22 citation markers out of the body and collapsed the reference list into one paragraph with a stray "99" in it. The text looked fine at a glance and cited nothing. We rebuilt it from the source as v6 and v6.1, checked every marker against its reference, and only then submitted it to Zenodo and SSRN.

The same day I submitted Papers 1, 5 and 7 to SSRN and set up ORCID with all eight papers. Two weeks later SSRN approved five of them.
